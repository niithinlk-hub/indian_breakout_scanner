Strategy blueprint v1.0 · Historical backtest

MEDHAALPHA EMA 20/50 adaptive trend system

NSE single-stock F&O universe (177–226 stocks, point in time) · 18 Nov 2024 – 13 Nov 2026 · ₹3,00,000 account, ₹3,000 risk per trade · run 16 Nov 2026

Verdict: no tested version earns its way to paper trading

Across 696 entry, timeframe, filter and exit combinations, the EMA 20/50 signals showed no edge that survives Indian trading costs. None of them met the blueprint's own promotion criteria out of sample. Traded together on the ₹3 lakh account, the best configurations from the development period lost ₹52.9k (-17.6%) over the full two years. The walk-forward run, which chose a configuration on each training window and traded it in the next unseen window, lost ₹79.5k. Under the blueprint's ₹3,000 maximum-loss rule, stock options were almost never tradable.

A stricter "gated" version traded only configurations with positive development expectancy after costs. It returned -₹17.1k under the ₹75,000 position cap and ₹7.1k (profit factor 1.02) when the cap is read as MIS margin; its walk-forward version lost ₹23.6k. The blueprint's own fallback therefore applies: when neither engine qualifies, stay in cash.

What the data says

  • The signals are not better than chance. Random entries with the same stops and exits earned +0.065R (shorts) and -0.010R (longs) per trade before costs. EMA 20/50 signals averaged +0.051R and +0.018R. Stocks in this period tended to drift down during the session, which helps any intraday short. The signals capture little beyond that.
  • Costs are the binding constraint. The median intraday configuration kept a gross edge of +0.032R per trade. Brokerage, STT, exchange fees, GST, stamp duty and modelled slippage cost it 0.47R per trade under the ₹75,000 per-stock cap and 0.36R even with MIS leverage. 0 of 240 intraday configurations are profitable after costs; 2 of 72 positional ones are.
  • The ₹75,000 cap shrinks every intraday trade. Read as position value, it limits a 5-minute trade with a 0.5–0.8% stop to roughly ₹400–600 of risk, not ₹3,000. Fixed per-order charges then take a large share of each trade's R.
  • Better exits don't rescue it. 12 of 384 entry × exit combinations turned positive on development data, and the best t-statistic across every configuration was 1.18. After ~696 tests, results like that are expected from noise alone.
  • Stock options don't fit a ₹3,000 risk budget. One lot of a near-the-money stock option typically risks ₹22.0k; a debit spread risks ₹11.3k. Under the strict maximum-loss rule only 2 spread trades in two years were executable. Sizing on the underlying stop instead also lost money, mostly to bid-ask costs on four option fills per spread. The best positional idea, a daily short, can't be expressed as an equity trade overnight, so it never reached the account.
  • Adaptive timeframe selection helped but didn't flip the sign. Switching to 15-minute signals on noisy sessions beat a fixed 5-minute engine in 100% of comparisons, moving the average from -0.33R to -0.29R per trade.

Options instrument test

Each final signal was mapped to a real contract from the previous session's NSE F&O bhavcopy. Contracts had to be at least 7 days (intraday) or 14 days (positional) from expiry, to stay clear of physical settlement, and to have traded at least 10 lots. The strike was the one with delta nearest 0.55 inside 0.40–0.65, and debit spreads sold the liquid strike nearest the 2R target. Contracts were priced with their own implied volatility at the underlying's actual entry and exit prices. Bid-ask cost was 1–2.5% of premium per leg per side, plus option STT (0.15% from April 2026) and charges.

The strict rule caps the maximum possible loss of the structure at ₹3,000, as the blueprint requires. The relaxed reading sizes on the modelled loss at the underlying stop (maximum premium ≤ ₹30,000). It breaks the 1% rule and is shown only to check whether options would help at all. Halving the assumed bid-ask cost still leaves both relaxed variants losing money. Model check: on positional trades the model's close-to-close P&L correlates 0.95 (long options) and 0.90 (spreads) with actual bhavcopy closing prices.

Decisions for you

1. Don't move this design to paper trading

Paper trading tests execution, not edge. With no out-of-sample edge, 20–30 sessions of paper trading would only confirm costs. A redesign should come first.

2. Settle what the ₹75,000 cap means for intraday

As written (position value), it caps intraday risk at a few hundred rupees, so costs dominate. Read as MIS margin, results improve (₹7.1k over two years for the gated system, PF 1.02), but they are still far from the 1.25 bar.

3. Drop stock options from v1 at this capital, or change the risk rule

A ₹3,000 maximum loss doesn't fit ₹5–10 lakh stock-option contracts. Index options with smaller premiums, or a larger account, would be needed. Either is a separate study.

4. If you continue the research, test slower, cheaper signals

The least-bad results came from 15-minute and daily signals, short-side bias and wider stops, which all lower the cost per R. A daily-timeframe system with fewer, larger trades, or a different source of edge than EMA crossovers, is the more promising path. The random-entry benchmark built here should stay in every future test.

Data, method and limits

Prices
Upstox V3 historical candles (3-min, 5-min, daily), adjusted for splits and bonuses. 15-min and 60-min bars resampled from 5-min, anchored at 09:15. NIFTY 50 for market regime.
Universe
Point in time: in the previous session's NSE F&O bhavcopy, 20-day average traded value ≥ ₹20 crore, ≥ 200 sessions of history. That gave 177 to 226 stocks a day, matching NSE's published F&O counts. 274 symbols over the window, including names later dropped or renamed.
No look-ahead
Signals use completed bars only; orders become active on the next bar. Higher-timeframe and daily values join by bar end time. Sector strength uses point-in-time industry baskets that exclude the stock itself. Training trades whose exits fall in a test window are purged.
Fills
Simulated on 3- or 5-minute bars. A bar that touches both stop and target counts as a stop. Gaps fill at the open. A stop-order entry bar can only stop out. Closing-auction bars after 3 Aug 2026 are single prints. Slippage is 5 bps a side, +3 bps on stops; doubling it is a sensitivity case.
Costs
Upstox Basic plan, date-aware: brokerage, STT (option STT 0.15% from 1 Apr 2026), NSE charges incl. IPFT, SEBI fee, stamp duty, GST and DP charges. Same-day exits of delivery trades are taxed as intraday. Sources and dates are in docs/medhaalpha/COST_ASSUMPTIONS.md.
Earnings
Actual results dates until mid-2025 (Yahoo Finance); later quarters projected from the same quarter a year earlier (median error 4 days) with a widened blackout window.
Validation
Development 18 Nov 2024 – 15 May 2026 with four walk-forward windows; holdout 18 May – 13 Nov 2026. Four independent reviewers audited the code for look-ahead, fill, cost and accounting errors, with a separate verifier for each finding; every confirmed defect was fixed before these results were produced. 18 unit tests cover the simulator, costs and risk rules.
Known gaps
PEL (Piramal Enterprises, F&O until Jul 2025) is missing because its candles were retired after its merger; that is about 0.5% of the universe. Market-cap screening is implied by F&O eligibility, not applied separately. No order-book depth, partial fills or rejected orders were modelled. Option prices between closes are modelled (sticky-strike IV), not traded quotes.
